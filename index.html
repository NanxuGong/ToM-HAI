Does Theory of Mind Improvement Really Benefit Human-AI Interactions?

This paper investigates a central question for Human-AI Symbiosis: do gains on static Theory of Mind benchmarks actually transfer into measurable, user-perceived improvements in interactive, real-world conversations?

Abstract

From benchmark scores to real interaction value

Improving Theory of Mind (ToM) in large language models is widely believed to be critical for socially effective Human-AI interaction. This work challenges the assumption that higher scores on static ToM benchmarks necessarily imply better real interaction outcomes. The paper introduces an interactive ToM evaluation paradigm, systematically studies representative ToM enhancement methods across goal-oriented and experience-oriented tasks, and combines benchmark-based simulation with user studies. The empirical results reveal consistent gaps between benchmark gains, interactive effectiveness, and user-perceived quality.

Evaluation Gap Static benchmark gains are not a reliable proxy for interactive quality.
Generalization Gap Improvement patterns differ between goal-oriented and experience-oriented tasks.
Perception Gap Measured gains can remain below user-perceived quality thresholds.

Research Motivation

Existing ToM evaluations are mostly static, third-person, and multiple-choice. Human-AI interaction is first-person, dynamic, multi-turn, and open-ended. This mismatch can create a gap between benchmark success and real interaction quality.
